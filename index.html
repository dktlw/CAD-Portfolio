Portfolio

INTRO:

Taking an idea from an initial sketch to a physical object is what drew me to CAD design and additive manufacturing. I enjoy exploring how a concept can become something useful, tangible and carefully considered. Combining digital modelling with 3D printing, I develop ideas through iterative design, practical prototypes and hands-on testing.

Uni Powercell. The product I wish could exist

A pocket-sized power concept built around replaceable magnesium battery cells and a 220 V outlet. The modular design makes individual cells easy to replace, supporting repairability and a longer product life. It explores what a versatile portable power source could become as battery technology advances.

Warehouse safety cutter

I have been developing this safety cutter for use in warehouses and retail environments, where shrink wrap and strapping need to be removed every day.The design combines an easily replaceable blade with a protective housing for reliable, controlled cutting.More than 20 iterations have refined its shape and mechanism.Further testing will guide the final design and the next stage of development.

Revive. A cleaner, brighter future

This concept began as a water cooler and evolved into a broader design question: could future technology turn waste into clean water? Revive explores that possibility through product form and function. It is a forward-looking design study, with practical feasibility dependent on advances in recycling and water treatment.

A safer piñata design

A pull-to-open piñata with a concealed release lever connected to a cord. A playful alternative to striking a traditional piñata.

A hand-sewn outer cover conceals the mechanism and keeps the contents securely inside until the release is pulled.

Custom keyboard housing. A cohesive design

This custom housing was designed to give a supplementary keyboard the same visual character as the main keyboard. The project focused on matching its proportions, colour and overall style while retaining the existing keyboard hardware. The finished enclosure brought the two devices together as a cohesive setup and achieved the intended result in everyday use.

USB-powered LED sign letters

A business in Grimsby commissioned a custom LED sign for its shop window. The project combined individually designed letter housings with integrated lighting and USB power. After assembling the sign, soldering the connections and testing the complete system, it was ready for installation. The finished sign gave the window a distinctive, illuminated focal point, and the owners were delighted with the result.
